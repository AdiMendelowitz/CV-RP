# Reliable Evaluation of Adversarial Robustness with an Ensemble of Diverse Parameter-free Attacks

Croce, Hein. ICML 2020. [arXiv:2003.01690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01690)

This paper addresses a reliability problem in the field: reported robustness depends on how the evaluating attack was configured, and its step size, iteration count, restarts, and choice of loss are effectively free parameters that authors could tune, sometimes unknowingly, in a defence's favour. AutoAttack removes that freedom by fixing a standardised, parameter-free ensemble and evaluating every model under it.

Two components address weaknesses of ordinary PGD. The first is Auto-PGD (APGD), a PGD variant that removes the step-size choice: it sets the schedule automatically from the loss progress, reducing the step size when the attack stops improving, and uses a momentum term and restarts. The second is a change of loss. Cross-entropy can be weakened by rescaling the logits, so APGD is also run with the Difference of Logits Ratio (DLR) loss, built from logit differences and invariant to shifting or rescaling the logits, which makes it robust to that failure mode. The standard ensemble is four attacks chosen for diverse failure modes: APGD with cross-entropy, targeted APGD with DLR, targeted FAB (a minimum-distortion attack), and Square Attack (a gradient-free black-box attack). Because Square uses no gradients, it exposes defences whose apparent robustness comes from gradient masking, which the gradient-based members can miss.

The accomplishment is empirical and methodological. Re-evaluating a large set of previously published defences, more than fifty models, AutoAttack lowered the reported robust accuracy in nearly every case, often substantially, showing how much prior evaluation had overstated. It became the default protocol behind the RobustBench benchmark and the field's standard for reporting robustness. The underlying lesson generalises: an evaluation with free parameters will be tuned by the party being evaluated, so a trustworthy benchmark must remove its own degrees of freedom.
