# Obfuscated Gradients Give a False Sense of Security

Athalye, Carlini, Wagner. ICML 2018. [arXiv:1802.00420](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00420)

This paper audits nine defences from ICLR 2018 that claimed white-box robustness and shows that most of them owe their apparent robustness to obfuscated gradients: they corrupt the gradient signal an attacker relies on, without removing the underlying vulnerability. A standard attack, run directly, cannot see through the obfuscation and reports a strong robustness number, which does not reflect the true decision surface.

The paper's main contribution is a taxonomy of three mechanisms and a matching counter for each. Shattered gradients come from non-differentiable or numerically unstable operations, and are defeated by Backward Pass Differentiable Approximation (BPDA), which replaces the problematic operation with a differentiable approximation on the backward pass only while keeping the true function on the forward pass. Stochastic gradients come from randomised defences, and are defeated by Expectation Over Transformation (EOT), which attacks the expected loss by averaging gradients over the randomness. Vanishing or exploding gradients come from very deep or iterative defence computations, and are handled by reparametrising or attacking the underlying function directly. The paper also gives a short behavioural checklist for detecting obfuscation, for example one-step attacks outperforming iterative ones, or black-box attacks outperforming white-box ones, signatures that should not occur against a genuinely robust model.

Applying these techniques, the authors circumvent seven of the nine defences, six fully and one partially, each within the threat model the original paper proposed, reducing reported robustness to near zero in most cases. Of the two defences that did not rely on obfuscated gradients, they name Madry-style PGD adversarial training as the one that genuinely raises robustness within its threat model. The lasting result is not the specific defences broken but the vocabulary and the diagnostic method, which remain the standard way the field reasons about untrustworthy robustness claims.
